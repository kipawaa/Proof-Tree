\subsection{Group Identity is Unique}\label{articles:algebra:group-theory:group-identity-is-unique}
The \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group-identity]{Identity} element $e$ of a \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group]{Group} $G$ is unique.

\subsection{Explanation}
There is only one element $e \in G$ satisfying $a \cdot e = e \cdot a = a, \forall a \in G$.

\subsection{Proof(s)}
Suppose there exist two \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group-identity]{Group Identity} elements, $e$ and $e'$.\
Then

$$\begin{align*}
e &= e \cdot e' & \text{since $e'$ is a \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group-identity]{Group Identity}}\\
&= e' & \text{since $e$ is a \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group-identity]{Group Identity}}
\end{align*}$$

Hence $e = e'$, as wanted.
