\subsection{Statement}
The *inverse of a product* of elements $ab$ of a \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group]{Group} $G$ is $b^{-1} a^{-1}$.

\subsection{Explanation}

\subsection{Proof(s)}
Let $a, b \in G$ and hence $ab \in G$ by closure.\
By definition of inverse, we have that $(ab)(ab)^{-1} = e$.\
Notice that

$$\begin{align*}
(ab)(b^{-1} a^{-1}) &= a(bb^{-1})a^{-1}\\
&= a e a^{-1} & \text{by definition of inverse}\\
&= a a^{-1} & \text{by definition of identity}\\
&= e & \text{by definition of inverse}
\end{align*}$$

Since \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group-inverse-is-unique]{Group Inverse is Unique} we have that $(ab)^{-1} = b^{-1} a^{-1}$, as wanted.
