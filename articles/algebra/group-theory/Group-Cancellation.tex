\subsection{Group-Cancellation}\label{articles:algebra:group-theory:group-cancellation}
If $a, b, c$ are elements of a \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group]{Group} $G$, then $ba = ca \implies b = c$, and $ab = ac \implies b = c$.

\subsection{Explanation}

\subsection{Proof(s)}
Let $a, b, c \in G$ and suppose $ab = ac$.\
Since $G$ is a \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group]{Group}, all elements have inverses - particularly $a$.\
Let $a^{-1}$ be the inverse of $a$.\
Then

$$\begin{align*}
ab &= ac\\
\iff a^{-1} a b &= a^{-1} a c & \text{left multiplying by $a^{-1}$}\\
\iff b &= c
\end{align*}$$

and

$$\begin{align*}
ba &= ca\\
\iff baa^{-1} &= caa^{-1} & \text{right multiplying by $a^{-1}$}\\
\iff b &= c
\end{align*}$$

as wanted.
