\subsection{Statement}
The *inverse* of an element $a$ in a \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group]{Group} $G$ is unique.

\subsection{Explanation
There exists exactly one inverse for a given element $a$ of a \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group]{Group} $G$.

\subsection{Proof(s)}
Let $G$ be a \hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group]{Group} and let $a \in G$.\
Suppose that both $b$ and $b'$ are inverses of $a$.\
Then

$$
\begin{align*}
e &= e\\
ab &= ab' & \text{since $b, b'$ are inverses of $a$}\\
b &= b' & \text{\hyperref[articles:algebra:group-theory:group-cancellation]{cancelling} $a$}
\end{align*}$$

as wanted.
